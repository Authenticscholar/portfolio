About

Alhassan Abdulai works at the intersection of physical climate risk, geospatial analysis, and adaptation policy. He builds evidence on drought, rainfall, and temperature trends, translates projections into decision-ready formats, and supports stakeholders who need defensible risk information—not speculative narratives.

His training spans an Erasmus Mundus MSc (Distinction, GPA 3.73) at Central European University, the University of the Aegean, and the University of Manchester, an MSc in Climate Change Engineering from PAUWES, and a First Class BSc in Agribusiness with a climate and food-security focus. That arc combines climate modelling, GIS, quantitative methods, and field delivery experience.

He has co-authored peer-reviewed work on African climate hotspots and adaptation/disaster-risk reduction, produced policy briefs on drought and food security in northern Ghana, and delivered GIS-based environmental risk assessments for multilateral programme portfolios. His outputs are designed for planners, researchers, and funders who need clarity under uncertainty.
